Every growing business reaches the point where "the person who is good with computers" is no longer enough. The next step is usually one of three options: hire an in-house IT team, outsource to a **managed service provider (MSP)**, or combine the two.

There is no single right answer. This guide compares the options so you can choose what fits your business.

## The in-house option

An in-house IT person or team works only for you. They know your people, your systems and your quirks, and they are physically present when something breaks.

**Strengths**

- Immediate, on-site presence and deep knowledge of your business.
- Full control over priorities day to day.

**Limitations**

- **Coverage:** one or two people cannot cover nights, weekends, leave and sickness.
- **Breadth of skills:** modern IT spans networking, servers, cloud, security, backup and end-user support. Few individuals are strong in all of them.
- **Cost and retention:** salaries, training, certifications and tools add up, and skilled IT staff are in high demand.
- **Key-person risk:** when someone leaves, a lot of undocumented knowledge leaves with them.

## The managed services option

A managed service provider takes responsibility for your IT under an agreed **service level agreement (SLA)**, usually for a predictable monthly fee.

**Strengths**

- **A whole team, not one person**, with specialists in networking, security, infrastructure and support.
- **Proactive monitoring** of servers, network and security, so many problems are fixed before users notice.
- **Predictable costs** and clear response and resolution times written into the contract.
- **Documentation and process:** a good provider documents your environment, which reduces key-person risk.

**Limitations**

- Less day-to-day presence unless on-site visits are part of the agreement.
- Quality varies a lot between providers, so choosing well matters.

## At a glance

| | In-house team | Managed services | Co-managed (both) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Office hours, limited by headcount | Extended or 24/7, depending on SLA | Extended, with local presence |
| **Skills** | Limited to the people you hire | Broad specialist team | Broad, plus internal knowledge |
| **Cost** | Salaries, training, tools | Predictable monthly fee | Mix of both |
| **Monitoring** | Often reactive | Proactive by design | Proactive |
| **Best for** | Large or highly specialised environments | Small and mid-sized organisations | Growing organisations with an IT lead |

## The best of both: co-managed IT

Many organisations land in the middle. They keep an internal IT lead who knows the business and handles day-to-day priorities, and use a managed service provider for monitoring, security, out-of-hours cover, projects and specialist skills. The internal team is freed from firefighting, and the business gets depth and resilience it could not afford to build alone.

## Questions to ask any managed service provider

If you are considering managed support, these questions separate strong providers from weak ones:

1. **What are your response and resolution times**, and are they written into the SLA?
2. **What do you monitor**, and what happens when an alert fires at 2am?
3. **Do you provide on-site support**, and how quickly can an engineer be at our office?
4. **How do you handle security**, including patching, endpoint protection and backups?
5. **What reports will we receive**, and how often will we review service together?
6. **Will you document our environment**, and do we own that documentation?
7. **What happens if we leave?** A good provider makes the exit clean and hands everything over.
