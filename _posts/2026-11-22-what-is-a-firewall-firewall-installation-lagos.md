You have probably heard someone say "the firewall is blocking it" or "we should check the firewall", and nodded along. But what is a firewall, exactly, and is the small box in your server cupboard doing anything useful? Many Nigerian businesses either have no proper firewall, or have one that was installed years ago and never looked at again.

This guide explains what a firewall does in plain terms, the different types, and how to decide what your business needs. If you are weighing up firewall installation in Lagos, it will also give you the right questions to ask.

## What a firewall actually does

A **firewall** is a security device or program that sits between your network and the outside world, usually the internet. It looks at the traffic trying to go in and out and decides, based on rules, whether to allow or block it.

Think of the reception desk at a well-run office. Visitors are asked who they are and who they are here to see. Some are let in, some wait, and some are turned away. Staff leaving the building are not stopped, but a stranger carrying out a server would be. A firewall plays that role for data.

In practice, a firewall can:

- Block connections from the internet that nobody inside asked for.
- Stop staff computers from reaching known malicious websites.
- Limit which programs and services are allowed to talk to each other.
- Record what happened, so that you can investigate after an incident.

## Firewall vs antivirus: not the same thing

These two are often confused. **Antivirus** (or **endpoint protection**) runs on each laptop or server and looks for harmful files and behaviour on that device. A firewall guards the network edge and controls the traffic between networks. One protects the building entrance, the other protects individual rooms. A business needs both, because an attacker who slips past one can still be caught by the other.

## Types of firewall

### Hardware firewalls

A physical appliance installed where your internet connection enters the office. It protects every device behind it, including those that cannot run security software, such as printers and CCTV recorders. Vendors you may meet include Sophos, Cisco, Huawei and Aruba, among others.

### Software firewalls

Built into Windows and other operating systems, or installed on a server. They protect one machine at a time. They are a useful extra layer but are not a replacement for a network firewall.

### Cloud-delivered protection

Some services filter traffic before it reaches you. Cloudflare, for example, is widely used to shield websites and online services from malicious traffic. This suits organisations with web applications, customer portals or e-commerce.

### Next-generation firewalls

You will see this term on quotations. A **next-generation firewall (NGFW)** goes beyond simple allow-or-block rules. It understands which application is generating the traffic, can inspect it for threats, filter websites by category and identify users rather than just devices. For most businesses with more than a handful of staff, this is the sensible kind to buy.

## What a firewall cannot do

A firewall is not a magic shield. It will not stop:

- A staff member typing their password into a fake sign-in page. See our [phishing checklist for your staff](/blog/how-to-spot-a-phishing-email-checklist/).
- Malware that arrives on a USB drive or a personal phone.
- Attacks using stolen passwords where no second step, such as multi-factor authentication, is required.
- Problems caused by a firewall that is badly configured or out of date.

That last point matters most. A firewall left on factory settings, with an easy admin password and years of missed updates, can be worse than none, because it gives a false sense of security.

## Signs your firewall needs attention

- Nobody can say who manages it or when it was last updated.
- The device is no longer supported by its manufacturer.
- The admin login still uses a default or shared password.
- Rules were added for old projects and never removed.
- Remote access ports are open to the internet without a clear reason.
- You rely on the basic router supplied by your internet provider as your only protection.

## Firewall installation in Lagos: what to ask before you buy

1. **How many users and devices will it protect?** The device must handle your real traffic without slowing the office down.
2. **Does it include security subscriptions?** Threat updates and web filtering usually need a yearly licence. Ask for the total cost over three years, not just the hardware price.
3. **Can it support two internet links?** With fibre cuts and provider outages a fact of life in Lagos, a firewall that switches automatically to a backup link keeps people working.
4. **Who will manage it?** Rules, updates and log reviews need regular attention.
5. **Will it protect remote workers and branches?** Many firewalls can create secure VPN connections between sites.
6. **Is it on stable power?** A firewall that reboots with every power flicker is a weak point. Put it on a UPS, as we explain in our guide to [keeping your server room running through power cuts and heat](/blog/keeping-your-server-room-running-through-power-cuts-and-heat/).

## A simple routine for keeping a firewall effective

- Apply firmware and security updates on a schedule.
- Review the rules every quarter and delete what is not needed.
- Use a strong, unique administrator password and turn on multi-factor authentication for the management page where it is offered.
- Back up the configuration, so a replacement can be set up quickly.
- Check the logs, or have your IT provider do so and report to you.

## Do you need one?

If you have an office network, store customer or staff data, or take payments, the answer is yes. A firewall is also part of what regulators expect when they look at whether you have taken reasonable technical steps to protect personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023.
