Many business owners hear "managed IT services" and are unsure what they would be paying for. Is it a helpdesk? Monitoring? A monthly retainer that covers everything? The honest answer is that it depends on the provider and the contract, which is why two quotes for **managed IT services in Nigeria** can look very different.

This guide explains what is normally included, how pricing usually works, and what to check so that you buy cover you actually need. If you are still deciding between outsourcing and hiring, read our comparison of [managed IT support and an in-house team](/blog/managed-it-support-vs-in-house-it-team/) first.

## What managed IT services mean

A **managed service provider (MSP)** takes ongoing responsibility for part or all of your IT, under a written agreement called a **service level agreement (SLA)**. The SLA states what is covered, how quickly the provider responds, and how problems are escalated.

The key difference from ordinary "call us when it breaks" support is that the provider works to prevent problems as well as fix them. You pay for a service and an outcome, not just for hours of an engineer's time.

## What is usually included

Scope varies, but a solid package normally covers the following.

### Helpdesk and end-user support

Staff can report problems by phone, email or a ticketing system and get a logged, tracked response. Good providers offer **remote support** for quick fixes, so a locked account or a misbehaving application does not wait for a site visit.

### Proactive monitoring

Software watches servers, network equipment and key services for warning signs such as a failing disk, a full server drive or a link that keeps dropping. This is what **proactive IT monitoring** means: engineers are alerted and act before users notice. Tools such as ManageEngine are examples of platforms used for this kind of monitoring and ticketing.

### Patching and maintenance

Operating systems and applications need regular security updates. A managed service schedules and applies them, and carries out routine preventive maintenance such as checking equipment health and cleaning up user accounts.

### Backup oversight

The provider checks that backups run, alerts you when they fail, and tests restores. This is only useful if the backups themselves are designed well, so see our guide to the [3-2-1 backup rule](/blog/3-2-1-backup-rule-for-business/).

### Security basics

Typically this includes endpoint protection on laptops and servers, email filtering, firewall management and account controls such as multi-factor authentication. Deeper work, such as penetration testing, is often a separate service.

### Asset and licence tracking

A register of what you own, who uses it, and when warranties and software subscriptions expire. This prevents surprise renewals and unlicensed software.

### Reporting and service reviews

A monthly or quarterly report on tickets, response times, incidents and recommendations, ideally reviewed together with someone from your management.

## How managed IT services are priced

Providers generally use one of four models. Ask which one you are being quoted.

- **Per user per month:** a fixed fee for each staff member supported. Simple to budget, and it scales as you grow.
- **Per device per month:** a fee for each laptop, desktop or server covered. Suits organisations with many shared devices.
- **Tiered packages:** bundles such as basic, standard and premium, differing in hours of cover, response times and included services.
- **Time and materials:** you pay for hours worked. Flexible, but costs are unpredictable, and it rewards fixing rather than preventing.

Most fixed-fee contracts are quoted in naira or tied to a dollar rate, so ask which applies and how often the price can change. Equipment and licences bought through the provider are normally billed separately from the monthly service fee.

## What drives the cost up or down

- **Number of users and devices.**
- **Hours of cover:** office hours only costs less than evenings, weekends or 24/7 cover.
- **On-site visits:** whether engineers visiting your premises are included or charged per visit. This matters for branches outside Lagos.
- **Age and condition of your equipment:** a neglected environment costs more to stabilise, often through a one-off onboarding or clean-up project.
- **Complexity:** several offices, servers, cloud services and regulated data all add work.

Do not choose purely on the lowest figure. A cheap contract with slow response times and no monitoring can cost far more in downtime than a better one.

## What is often not included

Read the exclusions carefully. Common ones are new hardware, major projects such as an office move or a Microsoft 365 migration, cabling work, third-party software support, and incidents caused by something outside the agreement. None of these is unreasonable, but you should know about them in advance.

## A checklist before you sign

1. Is the **scope** written down, with a list of what is and is not covered?
2. Are **response and resolution times** defined for each priority level?
3. What are the **support hours**, and what happens outside them?
4. Is **on-site support** included, and how quickly can an engineer reach you?
5. How are **power and connectivity failures** handled for your site?
6. Who owns the **documentation** and admin passwords, and can you have them if you leave?
7. What is the **contract length and notice period**, and how are price changes handled?
8. What reports will you receive, and how often will you meet?
9. How will the provider protect your data, in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023?

## Getting started

Begin by listing your users, devices, servers, key applications and the problems that cost you the most time. That alone makes quotes easier to compare, because every provider is pricing the same thing.
